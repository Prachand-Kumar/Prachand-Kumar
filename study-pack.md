## High-yield study material

### Physics

**Electricity:** Current is the rate of flow of charge: I = Q/t. Heating effect: H = I^2Rt. A fuse is connected in series and melts when current becomes unsafe. A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field; its direction is found by the right-hand thumb rule.

**Pressure and winds:** Pressure = force/area. Liquids and gases exert pressure in all directions. Uneven heating of Earth creates pressure differences, causing winds. Warm air rises and cooler air moves in. Cyclones gain energy over warm ocean water; follow official warnings and avoid exposed areas.

**Light:** Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection. A plane mirror forms a virtual, erect, laterally inverted image of the same size. Concave mirrors can form real or virtual images; convex mirrors give a wide field of view. A convex lens converges light; a concave lens diverges it.

**Motion:** Speed = distance/time; velocity includes direction. Acceleration = change in velocity/time. Slope of a distance-time graph gives speed; slope of a velocity-time graph gives acceleration; area under a velocity-time graph gives displacement.

**Force and motion:** Newton's first law explains inertia. F = ma. Momentum p = mv, and force equals rate of change of momentum. Action and reaction are equal and opposite but act on different bodies.

**Work and energy:** Work W = Fs when force and displacement are along the same direction. Kinetic energy = 1/2 mv^2. Potential energy near Earth = mgh. Power = work/time. Energy changes form but total energy is conserved.

### Chemistry

**Matter:** Matter has mass and occupies space. Particles have spaces between them, move continuously and attract one another. Diffusion becomes faster at higher temperature. Changes of state involve latent heat; temperature remains constant during melting or boiling.

**Elements, compounds and mixtures:** An element contains one type of atom. A compound has elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio and has new properties. A mixture has variable composition; components retain their properties and can usually be separated physically.

**Solutions:** The solvent dissolves the solute. A saturated solution cannot dissolve more solute at a given temperature. Concentration can be expressed as solute amount per amount of solution. Solubility of many solids increases with temperature, but gases usually become less soluble.

**Separation:** Use filtration for an insoluble solid, evaporation or crystallisation for a dissolved solid, separating funnel for immiscible liquids, distillation for a solvent or liquids with sufficiently different boiling points, and chromatography for coloured components.

**Atom:** The nucleus contains protons and neutrons; electrons occupy shells. Atomic number Z = number of protons. For a neutral atom, electrons = protons. Mass number A = protons + neutrons. Isotopes have the same Z but different A.

### Biology

**Health:** Health includes physical, mental and social well-being. A balanced diet, exercise, sleep, hygiene, vaccination and clean surroundings reduce disease risk. Communicable diseases spread through pathogens; non-communicable diseases do not spread person to person.

**Nature in harmony:** An ecosystem contains interacting biotic and abiotic components. Producers capture solar energy, consumers obtain food from other organisms, and decomposers recycle nutrients. Energy decreases at successive trophic levels.

**Earth:** Liquid water, a suitable temperature range, atmosphere and solar energy make Earth suitable for life. The ozone layer reduces harmful ultraviolet radiation. Conservation protects soil, water, air and biodiversity.

**Cell:** The cell is the structural and functional unit of life. Plasma membrane is selectively permeable. Nucleus controls activities and stores genetic material. Mitochondria release usable energy. Ribosomes make proteins. Plant cells also have a cell wall, plastids and a large vacuole.

**Tissues:** Meristematic tissue causes plant growth. Parenchyma stores food, collenchyma gives flexible support and sclerenchyma gives strength. Xylem transports water and minerals; phloem transports food. Epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues are major animal tissues.

### Mathematics

**Coordinates:** A point is (x, y). On the x-axis y = 0; on the y-axis x = 0. Quadrants: I (+,+), II (-,+), III (-,-), IV (+,-). Distance on a horizontal or vertical line is the absolute difference of the changing coordinate.

**Polynomials:** A linear polynomial has degree 1. A zero makes the polynomial equal to zero. For ax + b, the zero is -b/a. Use the factor theorem: if p(k) = 0, then (x-k) is a factor.

**Numbers and powers:** Rational numbers can be written p/q; irrational numbers cannot. Real numbers include both. For non-zero a: a^m a^n = a^(m+n), a^m/a^n = a^(m-n), (a^m)^n = a^(mn), and a^0 = 1.

**Identities:** (a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2; (a-b)^2 = a^2-2ab+b^2; a^2-b^2 = (a-b)(a+b); (x+a)(x+b) = x^2+(a+b)x+ab.

**Mensuration:** Rectangle: P = 2(l+b), A = lb. Square: P = 4a, A = a^2. Triangle: A = 1/2 bh. Parallelogram: A = bh. Circle: C = 2 pi r, A = pi r^2. Keep units consistent.

**Probability and proportion:** For equally likely outcomes, P(event) = favourable outcomes/total outcomes, from 0 to 1. In direct proportion y/x is constant; in inverse proportion xy is constant. Convert fractions, decimals and percentages before comparing.

### Mental Ability

**Series:** Check differences, second differences, multiplication, alternating rules, squares/cubes and position-based letter jumps. Write the rule above each gap.

**Coding and alphabet:** Convert letters to positions A=1 to Z=26. Test shifts, reversal, pairing and word-value patterns. Verify the rule against every given example.

**Relations and directions:** Draw a small family tree for blood relations. For directions, sketch north-east-south-west and track each turn. Use coordinates for longer paths.

**Ranking and arrangements:** Total people = rank from top + rank from bottom - 1. For seating, place fixed clues first, then immediate left/right clues, and test remaining conditions.

**Spatial reasoning:** For dice, opposite faces never share an edge. For painted cubes, classify corner, edge, face-centre and inner cubes. In mirror images left-right reverses; in water images top-bottom reverses.
